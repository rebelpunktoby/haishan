風雨殿

Temple of Wind and Rain

A building played by weather. Wind in the walls, rain on glass, sun in the pitch. The building only tunes and listens.

The plan follows the text.

Headphones recommended. A browser sketch, not a recording.

i · Origin

A door in winter

New York. A northwest door, a narrow gap, a hard wind. The gap sounded. From across the room it was close to music.

ii · Wall

The wall as instrument

Wind carries little pressure, so the voices are large and low. A slot lets the air speak. A tuned chamber behind it fixes the pitch. Shutters close in a storm.

How it works

At 15–40 km/h, wind supplies 10–74 Pa. Length and volume set pitch. An open pipe overblows to the octave, a stopped pipe to the twelfth, so gusts stay within the chord.

iii · Pedal

One held note

Every wind face shares a low A, at 55 and 110 Hz. It does not change. All other pitches are colour above it.

Sun in the pedal

The high A sounds from two matched chambers, one sunlit, one shaded. Warmth raises pitch about 3 cents per °C. By day the pair drifts and beats slowly. At dusk it settles.

iv · Faces

Eight faces

One face for each trigram. The prevailing wind turns with the year: 震 in spring, 巽 in summer, 兌 in autumn, 乾 in winter. Over the same A, each face sounds its own chord.

The other four faces

離, south: light. 坤, southwest: entrance. 坎, north: water out. 艮, northeast: silence. Voicings shown are provisional.

Why it stays consonant

Roughness depends on the distance in hertz between two tones, and that distance is about the same in every register. Close intervals sit high or stay short. Low voices stay wide. Within that rule, any scale works above the pedal, microtones included.

v · Rain

Rain, gathered

Rain alone on glass is noise. Collected, then released as single drops from a height, it strikes tuned glass in the open skywell. The rhythm follows the storm.

How it works

A gathered drop carries the energy of roughly fifteen raindrops. Catchment size sets each voice's tempo. Drainage keeps the glass at pitch.

vi · Light

Light

Sealed slits on the south face. A blade of sun moves across the wall with the hour, and at noon its reach marks the season. Its heat bends the pedal, so the sun is also heard.

Figures

A 5 mm slit casts a blade that travels about 1.3 m per hour across a wall 5 m away. At 35° N, noon light reaches 0.6 m into the room at midsummer, 4.9 m at midwinter.

vii · Stillness

Stillness

On windless, dry days the building is silent. Silence belongs to the weather.

ix · Method

Measured, inherited, chosen, hoped

Each claim carries one label: measured, inherited, chosen, or hoped. The trigrams give the building its order. They make no claim about nature.

Open questions
  1. H1. Is tuned wind heard as more pleasant than untuned wind?
  2. H2. Does the spacing rule predict how rough a mix sounds?
  3. H3. Is the slow sun beat heard as calm, or as out of tune?

Roughness follows the Plomp–Levelt model. It measures one aspect of hearing, not musical meaning.

Principles
  1. Silence is shared weather.
  2. No health or therapeutic claims. Listeners are never sensed.
  3. Nature plays. The building tunes.
  4. One pedal, many colours.
  5. Spacing before scale.
  6. Each opening serves sound and light where it can.
