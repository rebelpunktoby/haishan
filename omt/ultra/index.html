Wind-Rain-Light Temple

A building played by the weather. Wind gives the breath, rain the touch, sunlight the slow turn of pitch. Nature plays. The building tunes and listens.

Use headphones or speakers with some low end; the pedal sits at 55 and 110 Hz. It starts softly. This is a browser sketch of the idea, not a recording of a building.

02 · Idea

It began with a door that sang in winter

In a New York winter, wind pushed through the gap of a floor-to-ceiling door that faced northwest. It made tones, in layers, and from across the room they sounded close to music.

Read the full idea

A door gap is the mouth of a flute; the room behind it is the body. Instruments are shaped so their sound fits the way we hear. Wind and rain have always made sound. This work shapes a whole building the same way, so that what the weather plays arrives as harmony.

OceanMountain 山海 is the digital work: sound drawn from the hour and the season. This is its body. Nothing here is generated. The weather is the performer, and silence is part of the weather.

03 · System

One held note, eight faces, three elements

A sounds low on every wind face, at 55 and 110 Hz. Each face adds three notes above 290 Hz. As the wind turns with the seasons, the same A becomes a fifth, a root, a third. The year modulates around one held note.

The other four faces

These voicings are placeholders. The other four faces do other work: 離 south takes the sunlight, 坤 southwest is the entrance, 坎 north is the shaded wall where water leaves, 艮 northeast is the silent wall. The trigrams are an organising grammar from tradition, not a claim about nature.

Wind

The slot lets it speak. The chamber sets the note.

How it works

Wind at 15 to 40 km/h supplies only 10 to 74 Pa of pressure, a fraction of what an organ uses. So the wind voices are large, low and led by their resonators, with shutters to hold them back in storms.

Rain

Rain, gathered to a single drop, falls on glass

How it works

Rain falling straight onto glass is mostly patter. One gathered drip carries the energy of about fifteen raindrops and lands on one chosen spot. The size of each catchment sets the tempo of its voice.

Light

Sunlight is seen, and also heard

How it works

The south face is cut with sealed slits. A 5 mm slit throws a blade of light that crosses a wall five metres away at about 1.3 m an hour. At 35° north, noon light reaches 0.6 m into the room in midsummer and 4.9 m in midwinter.

Sun also warms the air in a chamber and raises its pitch about 3 cents per °C. Two matched pedal chambers, one in sun and one in shade, drift apart and beat slowly by day, then still at dusk.

Harmony

Spacing before scale

How it works

The gap that sounds rough is nearly the same number of hertz in every register. Close intervals belong high or short; low voices sit wide. With that met, any scale works above the pedal, microtones included.

04 · Theory and method

What is measured, what is inherited, what is chosen, what is hoped

Every claim in this project carries one label. The labels decide what may be said about it and what would count as it failing.

MeasuredWind pressure, pipe lengths, pitch drift with temperature, roughness scores. Computed from standard acoustics; none yet measured on a built object.
TraditionalThe eight trigrams, yin and yang, 時辰 and 節氣. Used as a living grammar for organising the building, never as physics.
Design choiceA as the pedal, the voicing of each face, the double wall, the skywell. Owned by the author and open to revision.
HypothesisThat the result is good to sit and listen with. Offered, not promised, and testable.
Three questions the test panel can answer
  1. H1. Is tuned pedal-and-colour wind sound judged more pleasant than untuned wind tones?
  2. H2. Does the spacing law predict how rough listeners rate a mix?
  3. H3. Is the slow sun beat on the pedal heard as calm, or as out of tune?

The roughness figures use the Plomp–Levelt model. It covers one part of hearing and says nothing about musical meaning. A door-sized panel with one wind voice and five glass bars is the next step, with timed listening sessions.

Laws
  1. Silence is shared weather. Still, dry days are silent by design.
  2. No health or therapeutic claims. The listener is never sensed or profiled.
  3. Nature plays. The building only tunes and listens.
  4. One pedal, many colours.
  5. Spacing before scale.
  6. An opening does two jobs where it can: sound and light.

About
